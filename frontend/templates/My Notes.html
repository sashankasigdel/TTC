Grade 11 Economics - Chapter 3: Theory of Demand

This chapter covers the fundamental concepts of demand, demand schedule, demand curve, and factors affecting demand.

Progress: 3/8 pages

What is Demand?

Demand refers to the desire to buy a commodity backed by willingness to pay and ability to pay for it. In economics, demand is always effective demand.

Important: Demand = Desire + Ability to Pay + Willingness to Pay

Demand Schedule

A demand schedule is a table that shows the quantity demanded of a good or service at different price levels.

Price (₹) Quantity Demanded
10 100 units
20 80 units
30 60 units

Demand Curve

The demand curve is a graphical representation of the demand schedule. It slopes downward from left to right, showing the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

Qd = f(P) where Qd is quantity demanded and P is price

Factors Affecting Demand

  • Price of the commodity
  • Income of the consumer
  • Price of related goods (substitutes and complements)
  • Tastes and preferences
  • Expectations of future prices
  • Population size and composition

Law of Demand

The law of demand states that, other things remaining constant, the quantity demanded of a good increases when its price falls and decreases when its price rises.

Remember: The law of demand assumes ceteris paribus (all other factors constant).

Exceptions to the Law of Demand

  1. Giffen goods
  2. Prestige goods (Veblen goods)
  3. Goods with speculative demand
  4. Necessities
